# Expenses, Reimbursement, and Pay

Jordan

If the client employs you, you get a biweekly paycheck. If you are on your own, you pick the method. Cash. E-transfer. Pay by bill.

I am employed on the household job. That comes with paid leave, sickness pay, holiday pay. Tax comes on the stub. Self-employed means you file. Holidays and sick days are on you.

Full-time residential jobs can come with time off, dental, other benefits. They can also come with an obligation to work when you are sick. Maybe no pension. Maybe no healthcare. Chefs forget that part until they are in it.

## How the food money moves

I put groceries on my card, personal or company. I get reimbursed at the end of the night. I use a card on purpose. I keep the points. You should never eat grocery cost. Get it back.

Receipts live in Notion. I keep a chart, add the week, send the total. Reconciliation is a text with the grocery breakdown.

I have only used that card method. I have not run a cash float. I am not going to invent a disaster I did not have.

## Charge for the hours that are not cooking

Charge for shopping time. Shopping is labor. A lot of new private chefs skip the hours it takes to draft a menu, shop, and go back and forth with the client. Record every hour. Put it in the price.

Hidden cost on dinners is the same pile: shopping, planning, the messages. Build that into the final number.

The biggest money mistake I see is chefs who only bill the cook window. The week is longer than service.

## Contract and late pay

Put it in writing. Pay. How often you work. Days off. What the client needs to provide. Payment terms.

I do not let pay sit more than a week late. I have been paid late. Sign that they pay within a week after the event is finished. The contract is how you stop pretending it will fix itself.

I price household size, travel, type of food, how much food, extra labor if I need it.

## Rate

My rate went up because the execution went up. More years. A more premium service.

If you are new, look up chefs with your skill level and your years. That gives you a ballpark for your city. Prices can go up and they can come down. I still aim to charge more. It is easier to charge one person more than to charge ten people less for the same work.

I do not have a floor I tell everyone to stay above. Situations get tricky. Starting out you may not feel comfortable charging what the internet says. That is okay.

I have underpriced myself a lot. Imposter syndrome does that. You do not feel it in the moment. A couple years later you look back and ask why you charged that. Underpricing is not always a mistake at the start. I tell new private chefs to charge grocery reimbursement and no labor on the first dinner, because they have no experience in the house yet. That is school. After that you charge.

## The raise text I send

Asking for a raise still makes me uncomfortable. I do check-ins. I talk about how I have improved and how I add more value. That is when they give it.

Hi person I wanted to inform you that I'll be raising my prices I know that we have been working for a while now, However I have made advancements in my career and For me to do so I have decided to raise my prices I hope you understand and we can still work together.

You can negotiate gas, housing, car insurance, time off. If you are self-employed, treat the tax side carefully. I am not your accountant.

After my first private chef paycheck I saw the gap. I made more in one night than I could have made in a restaurant week. March 2026, dinner for six, payout plus a $200 tip, about five hours. That night I knew the money was real.

Charge for the shopping. Get the groceries back. Write the week down. Send the text when the work has actually gone up.
