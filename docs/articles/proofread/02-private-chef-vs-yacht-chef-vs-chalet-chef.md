# Private Chef vs. Yacht Chef vs. Chalet Chef

Jordan

I have been private cheffing about two years. Events and personal cheffing on top of that for about a year. I am in Toronto. Most clients are in the GTA. I am not live-in. I sleep at my own place.

I have not done yacht work. I can still tell you who should. Chalet work, in the way I have seen it, looks a lot like cottage country in Muskoka. Staff house. Estate manager. People living on the property. That is a different life than driving into a GTA house at 9 and leaving at 9.

## Household private chef

This is the lane I am in. One family. Same kitchen. Stove, barbecue, pizza oven, tools already there. Provisioning is predictable because you serve the same people.

My day starts around 8. Grocery store around 9. Breakfast at the house for 10. From 10 to 1:30 I prep lunch, make snacks, do baked goods. Lunch at 1:30. Break, usually a café nearby, more work. Back around 5:30 for the kids' dinner and adult dinner prep. Adults eat at 8. Clean up is 30 minutes to an hour.

Hours can run 9 to 9 when I do breakfast, lunch, and dinner. Breaks sit in the middle. Some clients only want dinner. Mine want the whole day.

Household work is more strenuous for me than events. You are the only one in the kitchen. That gets lonely. On paper I have two weeks off a year. I do not take them. I feel an obligation to feed the family even when I want a day off.

Pay, if the client employs you, is a biweekly paycheck. Some houses offer time off, dental, paid leave, sickness pay, holiday pay. Tax comes on the stub.

This lane fits people who want a stable routine and a paycheck. If you like talking to clients and building a connection, you will like the house. I am in a settled season. I met my wife two years ago. I wanted a family. I choose the household job for that stability.

## Events and private dinners

Menu, logistics, and the team get planned days ahead. I shop the day before and prep what I can. On the day I arrive about five hours early, finish, and serve when they ask. Clean up is the same window, 30 minutes to an hour.

You figure out the kitchen on the day. Bring your own tools. Adapt. Provisioning is heavier because each dinner is a new menu.

Events often need another person, so it feels less lonely. You usually charge a day rate or per head. Self-employed means you pick the method: cash, e-transfer, pay by bill. Contracts can be one night or they can stack.

If you want more creative work and more autonomy, you will like dinners and events more than a house. I still do them Thursday to Saturday. Monday to Wednesday I am in the house. People stack those two on purpose to make the week pay. It works if you stay on top of time and you do not bury yourself.

Hidden cost on dinners is shopping, planning, and the back-and-forth with the client. Build that into the price.

## Yacht chef

People who like to travel, long hours, and adventure can take yacht cheffing. People with a spouse, a family, and a routine they want to keep will hate the demand and the seasons that jump around.

I have not lived in a galley. I am not going to write you a fake day on a boat. The hire path I know is agencies and your own outreach. Yacht work sits with the same high net worth world as household and private dining. Those clients pay well. The lifestyle is the filter.

If you want to settle down, I would not start there.

## Chalet / cottage country

Some clients have a whole estate manager and household staff. With that comes a staff house. People live there. They bond with each other. You see a lot of this in cottage country, especially Muskoka.

That is the closest thing I have to "chalet chef." Live-in. Seasonal pressure. A property that is not a Tuesday night in the city. Some private chefs live on the property. Some do not. I do not.

You still need food safety, food handlers, Smart Serve, a police check. You still need a contract. Scope and borders still matter. Unexpected requests you never agreed to will wreck you in a house, on a boat, or in a cottage. Talk about that before you are already in the week.

I got the household job through Indeed and an agency. I got dinners through my own business. I reached out to people. Referrals started to stack.

Pick the lane that matches the life you are trying to build. I picked the house because I am building a family. The yacht can wait. Cottage country is there if you want the staff-house version of the same job.
