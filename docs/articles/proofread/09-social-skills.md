# Social Skills: The Part Restaurants Never Taught You

Jordan

I felt like an imposter when I was transitioning. I thought I did not have the skills yet. Clients care about hospitality, how you talk to them, how you handle a situation. Restaurant cooking does not teach all of that.

If you are a restaurant chef and you think you are not ready, look again at what you already know. It might be enough to start. The gap is usually the people part.

## The first day

Greeting the guest. Shopping. Managing my own time for prep. Managing my own time for cooking. In a restaurant someone else owns the clock and the front door. In a house you own both.

I had someone coaching me up. Side by side. I am thankful for that. Mentorship is how I would set a person up.

Being personable is what decided my hire more than the cooking history. They want to know if you vibe with the family. Resume gets you the meeting. The hello keeps you in the room.

## Where to stand

I do not think you need to be invisible. You need enough sense to know where you should be.

If the family is in a serious conversation, I am not standing next to them. If guests are over, I talk to the guests and make them feel at home. That is the skill. In and out.

A salesperson at the door: I act like I am the owner. I do that to protect their identity. Discretion is social too. You do not post their faces. You do not post their kids. You ask before a face goes up. You cover addresses.

## The tea

If your client drinks tea after lunch, have water boiled so they do not wait. Anything they do not have to think about sets you apart. They thank you for that.

Restaurants do not schedule you to notice a tea habit. In a house there is no runner. You are the one who sees it.

## When the night goes bad

Four more people, one hour before dinner. You can cook for them. Dinner moves another hour. You say that out loud. Extra people come with extra cost. Communication is the social skill there. Silence is how you get blamed.

Worst night I have had: I cooked a bag of pasta for the kids. They are allergic to egg. I skimmed the list. I served it. They did not feel well. We had a conversation. I told them I did not look carefully. I thought the job was gone. They gave me a second chance. I told them it would not happen again, what I learned, what I would do from then on.

Every mess up is yours. There is no team to hide in. There is no big restaurant name that takes the blame. Attention to detail is a social skill when you are the only adult in that kitchen who touched the food.

## How you lose the room

I have seen chefs let go for misconduct. Treating staff and servers unprofessionally. Partying late. Showing up late. A bad attitude. Word of mouth is fast.

Nannies, house managers, PAs. You are one operation. If you treat the staff like they are below you, the family hears it.

Every time I meet a client I put on my best self. Walk in. Smile. Shake hands. Greet everyone. Go above and beyond.

Restaurant chefs transitioning into private chef practice proteins. Practice the door too. Practice the tea. Practice the apology when you get it wrong. That is the job they never put on the ticket.
