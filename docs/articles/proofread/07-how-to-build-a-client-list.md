# How to Build a Client List

Jordan

I did not sit down and build a list in a spreadsheet. I still do not keep a CRM. I should. The list I have came from four places: people I already knew, an agency house, people who found me online, and people I walked up to.

## Friends and family first

Tell them you cook private events, dinners, celebrations. Ask if anything is coming up. Do the first dinner at cost. Groceries paid. Labor free.

You get into the work. You build a referral network. You also find out if you want this life. Then you document it and you post it. That is how you gather credibility and more clients.

## Agency as one name on the list

The first full-time household came through Indeed and an agency. That is one client. A real one. Monday to Wednesday I am still in a house like that. Households of 4 to 7. High net worth.

Agencies will match you. They take 15 to 40 percent of year one. After a full-time placement I did not need them again. The rest of the names came from me.

## Go where the clients already are

Ideal client, for me, is affluent. They live in affluent communities. They go to high-end gyms, sports car dealerships, private jet places, luxury real estate agents. Get in contact. Promote the service as a gift those people can give their clients.

Ads get your name out. Instagram DMs have booked dinner parties for me. Cold messages work when you use their name and you spent a couple minutes on their page.

I was driving through an affluent community. I stopped and asked someone if they needed a private chef. I was cooking for them a couple days later. That name is on the list because I asked.

## After you have one house

A year in, people started asking me to cater. People asked for a private dinner. That is when the list started growing without me chasing every name.

One good event can throw you a bunch of referrals. Keep every client satisfied. You do not know who they are talking to. A bad night talks too.

If I cannot take a date I send it to someone on my team. If I have nobody I ask to rebook. A list dies when you no-show or you ghost a yes you cannot handle.

## Keep the list alive

I do not actively stay in touch. That is a hole. Recurring clients come back easier if you remind them. A birthday note that says they had a good night last time and asks if they want another one would be enough.

Facebook groups, Reddit threads, the Brigade community. Be in the rooms other chefs and clients already sit in.

Blur the faces. Cover the addresses. The list is private. If you post the house, you do not get invited back, and you do not get the cousin's wedding.

Restaurant chefs transitioning into private chef wait for a perfect posting. Text your aunt. Cook the birthday. Write the name down. Ask the next person. That is a client list.
