# How to Build a Resume (Private Chef)

Jordan

Clients ask for a resume. They ask for previous experience. Some of them mainly want to know if you vibe together.

I did not have private chef experience when I got the first job. I found it on Indeed. A chef in Toronto was starting an agency and he took me on. I think they hired how I am with people more than they hired the cooking history.

So if you are a restaurant chef transitioning into private chef, the resume is not a wall of tasting menus. It is proof you can cook, plus proof a family can live with you.

## What to put on it

Put the kitchens. I would put oyster boy, the restaurants in Toronto, Mississauga, Oakville, the internship at Alo. Sixteen hour days. Mussels. Grape skins. Chervil. Fine dining taught me discipline and attention to detail. That belongs on the page because a house still needs that. I would not write the page like I am applying to another Alo. I left that room on purpose. I connect more to food a family shares than to prestigious plates.

Put that you can do a full day alone. Shopping. Menu. Prep. Cook. Clean. Greeting the guest. That is the job. A restaurant resume hides that because other people owned those pieces.

Put certifications. Food safety. Food handlers. Smart Serve. Police check. A lot of houses want a contract, an NDA, a background check. You are often around the kids. If you already have the police check, say so.

Put households if you have them. Size matters. Mine are usually 4 to 7 people. High net worth. Full-time plus events. Do not invent titles you did not hold.

## What the paper cannot do

The food is a big part of the resume. The first impression is what decides it. Can you be personable. Can you connect. Can you sit with this family.

A stage is the live resume. Station clean. Dishes. Multitasking. Identifying flavors. Show the full skill set there. They cannot get that from a PDF.

I document the work and I post it. Faces blurred. No addresses. No family photos that give them away. Social is how people have contacted me for dinner parties. That is part of the resume now whether you like it or not.

## How I would start yours if you have zero private chef nights

Reach out to friends and family. Tell them you cook private events, dinners, celebrations. Do the first dinner at cost. Groceries paid. Labor free. Then write down what you made and put it up with the faces blocked.

That dinner is the first line you can add that is not a restaurant.

Agencies will ask for previous experience, a resume, and why you want the role. Then a call or a Zoom. Background checks. They verify with past employers. They send the packet to the house. The family interviews you or they run a trial. Agencies want people who have been doing this, because they are protecting their trust. If you do not have house experience yet, the friends-and-family dinners and the social proof are how you get something to send them.

## What gets you taken off the list

I have seen chefs let go for misconduct. Treating staff and servers unprofessionally. Partying late. Showing up late. A bad attitude. Word of mouth is fast. Your resume is also the last house you walked out of.

Discretion belongs next to your name. I answer the door like I live there when a salesperson shows up. I do that to protect the family. If your Instagram is full of client faces and street numbers, you do not have a private chef resume. You have a leak.

Write the kitchens you actually worked. Write the house skills you actually do. Then go stand in a kitchen with a family and let them see the rest.
