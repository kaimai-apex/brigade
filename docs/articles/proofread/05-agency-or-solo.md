# Agency or Solo?

Jordan

I have worked with one household hospitality agency. There are many of them.

You apply or they reach out. They want experience, a resume, why you want the role. Then a call or a Zoom. Background checks. They verify with past employers. They send the packet to the house. The family interviews you or they run a trial.

Household agencies take about 15 to 40 percent of your first year salary. Ask that number before you sign. Ask if the contract blocks you from other agencies.

Agencies want chefs who already have experience. They are protecting their trust and their image. They want people who have been doing this.

## What the agency gave me

My first full-time private chef job came through Indeed, through an agency. A chef in Toronto was starting his own and he took me on. We worked a family together. I learned the ropes there. I had someone next to me. I would not skip a mentor if I were you.

That placement was full-time, so I did not need the agency again after that. More of my work now comes from me finding clients.

## What I had to set up to go solo

I am based in Canada. I went through the CRA site and incorporated. About $200. Food handlers about $50. Payroll / accounting software about $100 a month. If people work under you, you need that software and you need contracts for them too. Insurance if you get bigger.

Admin is 1 to 2 hours a week. Menus. Schedules. Paycheck stuff.

The hardest part of being the business is other people. Paying them well. Respecting them. Giving them enough information to grow their own careers.

A year in, people asked me to cater. People asked for a private dinner. That is when I knew I could go direct.

## Which route pays

I do not think the route decides the client. If you mean who pays more, you are looking at high net worth and ultra high net worth people who already value private dining, household chefs, yacht chefs.

Agency gets you into a house faster if you have the resume. Solo gets you the dinners you go out and ask for. Friends and family. First dinner at cost. Instagram. Ads. Walking into the rooms affluent people already use: high-end gyms, sports car dealerships, private jet places, luxury real estate agents. Offer the service as a gift they can give their clients.

I stack the week. House Monday to Wednesday. Events Thursday to Saturday. The house paycheck is the stable piece. The dinners are the business I built after I knew I could do it myself.

Ask about the cut. Ask about the lock. Then pick. Restaurant chefs transitioning into private chef can use an agency as the first house and still build the solo list on the other side of the week.
