# How to Network

Jordan

My first clients came from reaching out. Friends. Family. People on Instagram. Social DMs for dinner parties. The first full-time house came from an agency. After that the network grew and people asked me for dinners.

Every client is valuable. Keep them satisfied. You do not know who they will refer. Same for a bad review. They talk too.

## The job that came from the street

I was driving through an affluent community. I stopped and asked someone if they needed a private chef. It worked. I was cooking for them a couple days later.

Cold messages work when you use their name and you looked at their page for a couple of minutes. Personal beats a blast.

## Other chefs

Chefs send me work because they trust my skill set and they trust the guest will have a good night. I send work out when I cannot take the date or I need hands. They handle prep and cooking. I handle the guest. If I cannot take a job and I have nobody, I ask to rebook.

I invested in a chef who was coming up. He staged under me until he was ready. He works with me now.

## The people in the house

Nannies. House managers. Personal assistants. Vendors. People treat those roles like background. You are one operation. Communicate. Vibe with them. If the staff side goes bad, the family feels it.

Suppliers: reach out, say you are a private chef, ask if you can work out a deal. Food, equipment, linen.

One good event can throw you a pile of referrals.

## How I stay in touch (and how I do not)

I do not keep a CRM. I should. I do not stay in touch on a system. I should. An automated birthday note that reminds them of the last dinner and asks if they want another one would be neat.

Facebook groups and Reddit threads are worth being in. Brigade is the community I want that energy in.

My socials are 50 percent top of funnel, 25 middle, 25 bottom. The content is Brigade. Restaurant chefs transitioning into private dining. Faces blurred. Permission before a face goes up. Addresses covered. No hint that lets someone track a client down.

## How you get a bad name fast

Show up late. Be disrespectful. Bring a bad attitude. Word of mouth is fast and it can hurt the business.

Every time I meet a client I put on my best self. Walk in. Smile. Shake hands. Greet everyone. Go above and beyond.

That is the network. Other chefs. The staff in the house. The suppliers. The family you already cook for. The stranger you had the nerve to ask.
