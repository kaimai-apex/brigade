# What Clients Actually Look For in a Private Chef

Jordan

They will ask for your resume. They will ask where you worked. Some of them sit there to see if you vibe together.

Food belongs on that resume. I am not pretending the cooking does not matter. The thing that decides it, in my experience, is whether you are personable. First impression. Can you connect with the family. Can you be hospitable. That is what sets you apart from other chefs.

Some clients require previous private cheffing. Some do not. I got in because a chef in Toronto was starting an agency and he took me on. I also think they hired how I am with people more than they hired my cooking history.

I staged for that agency and I nailed the trial because I could talk to the family and I could still execute. Station clean. Dishes getting done. Multitasking. Identifying flavors. A stage is where you show the full skill set. Show it. They cannot guess it from a PDF.

Paperwork depends on the house. A lot of them want a contract, an NDA, a background check, a police check. You are often around the kids. Expect that.

## What the house is like once you are in

There is no set schedule. Dinner can be set for 6pm. Things run late. The client has something to attend. The time slides back or it jumps forward. You roll with the punches and you make it work.

The loud version is four more people, one hour before dinner. You can cook for them. Dinner moves another hour. You say that out loud so they understand extra people come with extra cost.

Dietary needs stack more than they did five years ago. People are more health-conscious. Cooking one meal that covers everyone is hard. You still have to know how.

I do not think you need to be invisible. You need enough sense to know where to stand. If the family is in a serious conversation, I am not next to them. If guests are over, I talk to the guests and make them feel at home. Knowing where to be and where not to be is a private chef skill.

Discretion is part of the hire even if nobody puts it on the posting. A salesperson shows up at the door and I act like I am the owner of the house. I do that to protect their identity and the family. I do not post family photos. I do not post video where a face could give them away. Faces get blocked or blurred. Addresses stay off the internet.

## Consistency

I thought private cheffing meant I would cook the food I decided to make. Techniques I wanted on the plate. That was wrong. Clients like humble good food. Sometimes I am cooking chicken fingers and fries. Ego buster. It is not what you want to cook. It is what they want to eat.

For me the split is about 90 / 10. Consistency versus a little creativity. Most of the time they want the thing they already eat. Jamaican oxtail and chicken is the dish I have made a bunch of times. They love it. They ask for it almost every week. Some houses sit closer to 60 / 40. Depends on the person.

## The small thing they thank you for

If your client drinks tea after lunch, have water boiled so they do not wait. Anything they do not have to think about sets you apart.

I have seen chefs let go for misconduct. Treating staff and servers unprofessionally. Partying late. Showing up like the house is a bar. Word of mouth is fast in this industry.

If you are a restaurant chef transitioning into private chef, practice the hello as hard as you practice the protein. The plate gets you in the room. The vibe keeps you in the house.
