# Private cheffing was the first time I felt hope

Jordan

I found private cheffing by researching a job that paid more, gave me a better work life balance, and let me have some autonomy. I was looking for another way to use the same skill set and make a better living.

My reaction was that there was hope.

A lot of chefs walk into this industry knowing the deal. Low pay. Hard work. Grinding each day. Missing family milestones. Private cheffing was the first time I thought I could start a business that sustains a family and buys time back.

The transition was a net gain for me. More money. More time with my people. More time for myself. I did not pay for the jump with my relationships. I got relationships back.

Two drawbacks sit on that. You work by yourself, so learning can halt if you stop pushing. I tell chefs to stay on YouTube and to stage in restaurants when they can, so the technical side does not die. You also still miss some family events. You still work long hours. The internet glamorized this job. Life is better. It is still a service job.

I have not thought about going back. I got a taste of this and I want to scale the business. I want other restaurant chefs to make the same transition.

A lot of chefs stay because the restaurant is comfortable. I am proud I left. Trusting the process sounds soft until you are the one who checked the job board.

If you are in the rut, I want you to feel the same hope I felt when I first read what this job can be. Then I want you to treat it like a real path, not a rumor.
