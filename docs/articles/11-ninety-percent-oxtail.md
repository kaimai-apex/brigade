# Ninety percent oxtail. Ten percent showing off.

Jordan

I thought private cheffing meant I would cook the food I decided to make. Techniques I wanted on the plate. Food that was inspired by me.

That was wrong. Clients like humble good food. Sometimes I am cooking chicken fingers and fries. That was a big ego buster. It is not about what I want. It is always what the client wants.

Consistency versus creativity is about a 90 / 10 split for me. Most of the time they want the thing they already eat. If that is what they want, that is what you make. The 10 percent is where you get a flare. Something new, fun, creative. Some houses sit closer to 60 / 40. Depends on the person.

One dish I have made a bunch of times is Jamaican oxtail and chicken. They love it. They ask for it almost every week.

The biggest ego trap for a restaurant chef walking into a house is the same sentence. It is not what you want to cook. It is what they want to eat.

If you need the tasting-menu high, take a stage on a night off. In the house, the win is the oxtail hitting the table again and nobody thinking about you.
