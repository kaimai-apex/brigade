# My first private chef job was on Indeed

Jordan

I found the first job on Indeed. A chef in Toronto was starting his own agency and he took me on. We worked a family together. That is where I learned the ropes.

I was in a good spot. Someone was building an agency and I got a chance. I also think they hired my personality. Being personable got me in more than my cooking resume.

The first day was eye-opening. Greeting the guest. Shopping. Managing my own time for prep. Managing my own time for cooking. In a restaurant someone else owns the clock and the front door. In a house you own both. Nothing went smooth. I had someone next to me coaching me up, and I am thankful for that.

If you can get a mentor beside you for the first stretch, take it. Side by side is how I would set someone up.

I did the job every day for two months before I felt like I knew what I was doing. A lot of reps. A lot of mistakes. You keep going.

Now I have one full-time client and a couple of event clients. Monday to Wednesday I am in the house. Thursday to Saturday I open for events. High net worth households. Usually 4 to 7 people.

I wish someone had told me I can do it. I felt like an imposter. Clients care about hospitality, how you talk to them, how you handle a situation. Restaurant cooking does not teach all of that. If you think you do not have the skills yet, look again at what you already know. It might be enough to start.
