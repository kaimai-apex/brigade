# I left restaurants because I was getting married

Jordan

I met my wife two years ago. That is when I knew I had to get out.

I would not call it a breaking point. Nobody slammed a door. I sat with the job I had and I could see the next ten years. Long days. Every day. Missing birthdays. Not being able to pick my kids up from school. Building a family on a restaurant schedule. I did not want that life.

My wife is a big reason I felt the need to jump. Nobody helped me make the jump. I checked job boards for a private chef opening that did not ask for private chef experience. I decided on my own.

A lot of cooks in that spot start shopping for a better restaurant. I used to do the same thing. How do I level up on this line. How do I get into a better room. I was never asking how I get out of the room.

I want these articles for the person I was. Men and women, 22 to 30, still on the line, burnt out, looking for more money and a way out. If that is you, private cheffing is a job. I did not treat it like a job until I went looking for pay, hours I could live with, and some autonomy.

The honest reason I am writing this is not a brand story. A lot of chefs in the same rut do not even see private cheffing as a future. I want them to know it exists. Then I want them to show it to the person standing next to them on the pass.

Brigade is where I put the knowledge and the story of how I got here. If you are looking for a breakthrough out of the restaurant industry, this is the platform I wish I had when I was still thinking the next restaurant was the move.
