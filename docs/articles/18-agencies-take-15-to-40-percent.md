# Agencies take 15 to 40 percent of year one

Jordan

I have worked with one household hospitality agency. There are many of them.

You apply or they reach out. They want experience, a resume, why you want the role. Then a call or a Zoom. Background checks. They verify with past employers. They send the packet to the house. The family interviews you or they run a trial.

Household agencies take about 15 to 40 percent of your first year salary. Ask that number before you sign. Ask if the contract blocks you from other agencies.

Agencies want chefs who already have experience. They are protecting their trust and their image. They want people who have been doing this.

My agency placement was full-time, so I did not need them again after that. More of my work now comes from me finding clients.

A year in, people asked me to cater and to do dinners. That is when I knew I could go direct.

Better clients is a soggy phrase. If you mean who pays more, you are looking at high net worth and ultra high net worth people who already value private dining, household chefs, yacht chefs.

I do not think the route decides the client. The client decides the client. Agency gets you into a house faster if you have the resume. Solo gets you the dinners you go out and ask for.

Ask about the cut. Ask about the lock. Then pick.
