# I cooked egg pasta for kids who cannot have egg

Jordan

Worst day I have had as a private chef. I cooked a bag of pasta for the clients' kids. They are allergic to egg.

Random night. Kids' food. I skimmed the ingredient list. Egg was in the pasta. I served it. Shortly after they did not feel well.

We had a conversation. I told them I did not look carefully. I thought the job was gone. They gave me a second chance. I told them it would not happen again, what I learned, what I would do from then on.

Attention to detail is the skill. Every mess up is yours. There is no team to hide in. There is no big restaurant name that takes the blame.

I get angry about how restaurants treat chefs. Passion gets used. A couple dollars over minimum wage for 12 to 16 hour days is robbery. Willingness is not a reason to underpay. After my first private chef paycheck I saw the gap. I made more in one night than I could have made in a restaurant week.

Best day on the other side of that. March 2026. Dinner party for six. Payout plus a $200 tip. About five hours of work. That night I knew this is what I wanted to do, and I knew I wanted to teach other people to do it.

I still miss family events. I still work long hours. The job is better. The internet sells it cleaner than it is.

If you are a restaurant chef transitioning into private chef, I want two things from you after you read this. Consider the job. Show this to someone on the line with you.

Brigade is the place I put the story so you do not have to find it the hard way. I already found some of it the hard way. The pasta was enough.
