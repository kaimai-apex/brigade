# A household day vs an event day

Jordan

I have been private cheffing about two years. Events and personal cheffing on top of that for about a year. I am in Toronto. Most clients are in the GTA. I am not live-in. I sleep at my own place.

A household day for me starts around 8. Grocery store around 9. Breakfast at the house for 10. From 10 to 1:30 I prep lunch, make snacks, do baked goods. Lunch at 1:30. Then I take a break, usually a café nearby, and I do more work. Back around 5:30 for the kids' dinner and adult dinner prep. Adults eat at 8. Clean up is 30 minutes to an hour.

Hours can run 9 to 9 when I do breakfast, lunch, and dinner. Breaks sit in the middle. Some clients only want dinner. Mine want the whole day.

An event day is different. Menu, logistics, and the team get planned days ahead. I shop the day before and prep what I can. On the day I arrive about five hours early, finish, and serve when they ask. Clean up is the same window, 30 minutes to an hour.

The full-time kitchen already has the stove, the barbecue, the pizza oven, the tools. Events you figure out on the day. Bring your own tools. Adapt.

Provisioning is heavier on events. Each dinner is a new menu. A household is the same family. You can predict them.

Household work is more strenuous for me than events. You are the only one in the kitchen. That gets lonely. Events often need another person, so it feels less lonely. Some estates have a manager and a staff house. You see that a lot in cottage country, Muskoka.

I get more days off than I did in restaurants. I can make family gatherings. I still work holidays and guest nights. That is the service industry. On paper I have two weeks a year. I do not take them. I feel an obligation to feed the family.

I stack the week the same way a lot of chefs do. House Monday to Wednesday. Events Thursday to Saturday. It works if you stay on top of time and you do not bury yourself.
