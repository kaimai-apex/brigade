# Alo taught me discipline. It did not teach me how to love food.

Jordan

The hardest kitchen I have worked was my internship at Alo in Toronto. Sixteen hour days on your feet. Grinding.

I remember processing mussels, pounds and pounds of them. Peeling the skin off grapes. Standing there picking chervil.

Fine dining teaches you discipline and attention to detail. It also teaches you how much labor sits under a plate people pay a lot of money for. I respect that. I do not connect to that food the way I connect to a Sunday meal at grandma's.

Prestigious food never made a way for me to connect. Grandma's food is recognizable. Everybody comes together for it. It is cooked with love. At Alo I learned how far you can push a grape. I did not learn how a family looks when they share a table.

That is why I became a private chef. I get to see the reaction. I get to see a family bond over food.

If you are a cook who is good in a tight kitchen and still feel nothing when the plate leaves, I am not telling you fine dining is fake. I am telling you I needed a different room. Private houses gave me the Sunday table during the week.

Keep the discipline. Alo put that in me. Use it on food people already love, in a kitchen they already live in.
